## 2. Formats being demoted or demonetized
- Unedited AI narration over stock footage ("stock footage + robot voice"). [OutlierKit 2026, vendor]
- "Generic AI slideshow narration is being demoted." [Anijam 2026, vendor]
- YouTube policy: "Image slideshows, templated storylines ... with minimal or no narrative" and AI
  content "giving the impression of mass production" are not monetizable. [High: YouTube Help 1311392]
- Meta: stitched clips, narrating what's on screen, minor edits of others' posts. [High: Meta 2026-03-13]
- What is breaking out instead: AI production plus a consistent character, a real point of view and
  original research. [Anijam, OutlierKit, vendor]

## 3. Reference deep-dive: Ricks Clips (youtube.com/@RicksClips3000, checked 2026-11-05)
- "Hey I'm Rick! Just a regular guy who loves doing voiceovers." 144K subscribers, 147 videos, Shorts only.
- The curiosity stories are the hits: The Ancient Jar That Warned an Empire (31M), Why Perfect
  Railroads Don't Work (21M), The Job Nobody Wants in Japan (16M), The Window That Breaks Japan's
  Gambling Law (14M), Your Brain's Secret Idea Zone (14M), Why Sailors Carried Storm Oil (13M), What
  the Grave Bell Really Meant (11M).
- Earlier comedy voiceover skits mostly got 50K-1M views (a few 1M-4.5M). The pivot to stories is
  what scaled.
- Lessons: curiosity title of 3-7 words, a story with a twist, one recognizable character, bold word
  captions, a voice with personality. All 25 of its curiosity titles are in done-topics.csv so we
  never retell them.

## 4. Page ideas for Page #1 (original concepts)
Each idea is our own page. They borrow what works (format, pacing, title patterns), never content.

### A. Time Intern (Recommended)
- Logline: an unpaid intern at the "Department of Weird History" gets zapped into a different moment
  in history each episode and files a report on the strangest job, loophole or invention he finds.
- Mascot: "Benny" (placeholder): small round body, big round glasses, oversized lanyard ID, clipboard,
  a glitchy wristwatch time machine. Same outfit in every era.
- Signature: opens with the watch glitching ("Shift 37..."), closes with a dry line ("...and I still
  don't get paid.").
- Series: Worst Shift Ever (weird jobs) | Loophole Logged (clever rule-bending) | Lost Tech (ancient
  inventions) | Why It's Like That (origins of everyday things) | Lost Genius (Middle East, Asia,
  Pacific ideas, from Loy's Dubai and Philippines background).
- Why: a reason for the mascot to be in every era, humor for Facebook shares, "Shift #" numbering for
  binge and follows, a topic pool as big as history.
- Risk: the frame can repeat. Rotate series and reactions.
- Name options: Time Intern, The Time Intern, Odd Shift, Benny Through Time (no big page with these
  names found in a web search on 2026-11-05; confirm handles in the apps).

### B. The Hidden Why
- A tiny curious character ("Kip", question-mark cowlick) asks why everyday things are the way they are.
- Series: everyday objects, body and brain, food origins, travel, money.
- Why: evergreen, search-friendly. Risk: crowded, less drama.

### C. Loophole Lou
- A sly little character in a fedora explains history's smartest loopholes, hacks and heists.
- Why: highest curiosity and shares. Risk: smaller pool, crime and gambling topics can get limited ads,
  Ricks Clips has loophole hits, "loophole" is a known Max Fosh theme.

### D. Lost Genius (as its own page)
- Ancient ideas from the Middle East, Asia and the Pacific (windcatchers, Persian ice houses, Banaue
  rice terraces, Polynesian navigation). Real gap, narrower pull for a US-heavy audience. Better as a
  series inside A.

### E. Stickman long-form sister channel (YouTube)
- "Every ___ Explained" style stickman videos of 10-15 minutes, cut into Shorts. Uses the fastest-growing
  format for long-form ad revenue and the 4,000 watch-hour path. Higher script effort. Good as Page #2.

## 5. Title formulas (patterns, not copies)
1. Why [Normal Thing] [Has Unexpected Detail] -> "Why Coins Have Tiny Ridges"
2. The [Object] That [Huge Consequence] -> "The Board Game That Smuggled Escape Maps"
3. The [Person] Paid to [Weird Task] -> "The Woman Paid to Shoot Peas at Windows"
4. The [Place] That [Impossible-Sounding Fact] -> "The Fake Town That Became Real"
5. How a [Small Thing] [Fooled / Stopped / Saved] [Big Thing]
6. The [War / Job / Rule] That Lasted Only [Tiny Number]
7. What [Old Object or Custom] Was Really For
8. He Kept [Doing It] for [Number] Years
Keep titles 3-7 words, concrete nouns, no false promises.

## 7. Hooks and script timing
Hook lines (first 2 seconds):
- "In 1930s London, people paid a woman to shoot peas at their windows."
- "Look at the edge of a coin. Those tiny ridges stopped a crime wave."
- "This town was fake. Then someone built a store there."

| Time | Beat | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 0-2 s | Hook | Weird true claim + strongest visual |
| 2-10 s | Setup | Where, when, who. The mascot arrives |
| 10-35 s | Escalation | 2-3 beats: why, what went wrong, the twist |
| 35-48 s | Payoff | The reveal |
| 48-55 s | Button + loop | Dry one-liner that flows back to frame one |

## 8. Mascot design checklist
- Silhouette readable at thumbnail size. 4 colors + 1 accent. Flat 2D, clean outline.
- Turnaround, 10 expressions, 6 poses saved in brand/refs/.
- One prompt block reused in every Flow prompt. Saved as a Flow ingredient, called with "@name".
- Same outfit in every era. Only props and settings change.
